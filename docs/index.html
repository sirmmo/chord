Arc is being discontinued. Here is what Chord does about it.

Arc was the best idea browsers had in a decade. It is officially shutting down in December 2026, and its users need somewhere to take their Spaces. Chord is that somewhere.

The product

Arc has an end date

Its maker has announced that Arc is discontinued as of December 2026. After that, no updates, no sync, no Arc. Every Space you built there needs a new home before then.

Chord: open source under the MIT licence, so it cannot be discontinued out from under you. It ships a release most weeks, and if the project ever stalls you keep the source and the installers.
The data

Your Spaces live in someone's cloud

Arc sync goes through its maker's servers; a closed app, with your setup locked inside it.

Chord: everything is a JSON file you can read, export and hand-edit. Sync goes through two GitHub repositories Chord creates in your account. Private, password-locked spaces never leave your computer.
The platforms

Mac first, everyone else later

Windows arrived late, Linux never did. Switching computers meant switching browsers.

Chord: the same app on macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel), Windows and Linux, from the same release, on the same day.

An Arc that puts privacy first

Not just an ad blocker. These are on from the first launch, and each has a switch.

DNS

Encrypted lookups through Quad9

Every name lookup goes over DNS-over-HTTPS to a Swiss non-profit resolver that blocks malware domains and keeps no logs. Cloudflare, Mullvad or your own endpoint, if you prefer.

GPC

Global Privacy Control, always on

Every request carries a legally meaningful “do not sell or share my data”, and pages can see it. Third-party cookies are neither sent nor accepted.

HTTPS

HTTPS first, Shields on

Plain addresses open over HTTPS, with a fallback when a site has none. Shields block ads and trackers with EasyList and EasyPrivacy.

Aliases

A new e-mail address per site

Right-click any e-mail field: Chord asks SimpleLogin or Firefox Relay for a fresh alias, types it in and copies it. Your real address stays yours.

Switch in five minutes, before Arc goes dark

Your Spaces, pinned tabs, favorites and history come along. Nothing to export from Arc first, and nothing lost when it closes.

Install Chord

Download the build for your system below and open it. On macOS, right-click → Open the first time, because the builds are not notarised yet.

Import from Arc

File → Import from Another Browser → Arc. Chord finds Arc's sidebar file on its own and brings every Space with its name, emoji and colour, pinned and today tabs, and the favorites bar. Tick History too and the last 20,000 visits come with them.

Sign in to GitHub (optional)

Settings → Sync creates a private repository for your spaces, bookmarks and passwords, and a public one for the bookmarks you choose to publish. Any other computer picks it up from there.

Add what Arc had

Extensions from the Chrome Web Store, your Boosts as CSS and JavaScript, Air Traffic Control rules, and an easel per space. Your shortcuts are the same: ⌘T, ⌘L, ⌘D, ⌘⇧D, Ctrl+1…9.

And the things Arc never did

Built by people who use the browser all day, for the things a browser should have had already.

Shields

Ads and trackers blocked

EasyList and EasyPrivacy built in, on by default, one switch to turn off per site.

Passkeys · Passwords

A password manager with passkeys

Saved with the OS keychain, protected by Touch ID or Windows Hello, synced as a KeePass file you can open anywhere.

Smart pages

Players get a now-playing card with controls, calls on Meet, Zoom and Jitsi get mute, camera and leave, GitHub pull requests show their checks, all from the sidebar, from any space.

RSS

A reader and curated spaces

Subscribe to any site's feed from the sidebar, import OPML, and follow the people whose finds you trust in a space of its own.

Markdown

Notes and bookmarks per space

A notes page in Markdown for every space, bookmarks with tags and notes, private or published on your own page.

MCP

An AI's browser

A local MCP server lets Claude Desktop or any MCP client read your tabs, open pages and use the tools sites expose. Off by default, loopback only.

Music and video that play

Amazon Music, Spotify, Netflix and Prime Video work as in Chrome, through castLabs' Electron with the Widevine CDM.

Extensions

Chrome Web Store, one paste away

Install from a store address or id, or unpacked from a folder. Content scripts and most page APIs work.

Skins

Yours to theme

Any colour per space, tint, accent, icon style, font, light or dark, and an LCARS skin for the brave.

What to expect

Chord is young and honest about it.

  • Unsigned builds for now: macOS asks you to right-click → Open the first time, Windows shows “More info → Run anyway”. Signing is the next item on the roadmap.
  • Electron, not a fork of Chrome. Pages render identically; extension toolbar popups do not show, and extensions that lean on Chrome's own services do not work.
  • No phone app. Your public bookmarks are a web page you can open on any device; that is as mobile as it gets today.
  • A one-person project with a fast cadence. Something broken? Open an issue; most fixes ship within days.
  • Nothing leaves your computer unless you set up sync, and then it goes to repositories you own and can delete.
  • No company to be acquired. Chord is MIT-licensed code on GitHub, made by one person. There is nobody to sell it to and nothing to sell.
  • Not affiliated with The Browser Company, Arc or Atlassian. Chord reimplements the ideas, not the code.
