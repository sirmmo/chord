Welcome to Chord

Chord is a browser in the style of Arc, which only ever existed on the Mac. If you are coming from Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari on your system, four things are different, and the rest is the browser you know:

  • Tabs live in a sidebar on the left, not in a strip on top.
  • Spaces keep separate sets of tabs (work, home, a project) one keystroke apart.
  • A command bar replaces the address bar: one box for URLs, searches and commands.
  • Tabs tidy themselves: today's tabs are archived after a while unless you pin them.

Install and first launch

macOS

  1. Download Chord-<version>-mac-universal.dmg. One file runs natively on Apple Silicon and Intel, on macOS 13 or later. Still on macOS 12 Monterey? Take …-mac-universal-legacy.dmg instead: the same Chord on Electron 43, the last one that runs there.
  2. Open it and drag Chord to Applications.
  3. Chord is not notarised yet, so the first launch may be refused. Right-click the app and choose Open, or run xattr -cr /Applications/Chord.app once in Terminal.
  4. Updating: drag the new version over the old one. Your spaces and settings stay.

Windows

  1. Chord-<version>-win-x64-setup.exe installs for your user, no admin rights needed. SmartScreen will warn about an unknown publisher: choose More info, then Run anyway.
  2. Prefer no installer? Chord-<version>-win-x64-portable.zip unpacks anywhere, a USB stick included. While portable.txt sits next to Chord.exe, everything Chord saves goes to a ChordData folder beside it.
  3. Close with the window button or Ctrl+Q; the window's close button can also keep Chord running in the tray (Settings → General).

Linux

  1. Debian and Ubuntu: sudo apt install ./Chord-<version>-linux-amd64.deb, then Chord is in your app menu.
  2. Anything else: make the AppImage executable (chmod +x Chord-*.AppImage) and run it.
  3. Chord registers itself for chord:// links. Set it as the default browser from Help → Set Chord as Default Browser.

The sidebar

Top to bottom, the sidebar of a space holds:

  • Favorites: a grid of icons for the sites you open all day. They are shared by every space. Drag a tab onto the grid, or right-click it and choose Add to Favorites.
  • Pinned: tabs that stay until you close them. Cmd+D pins and unpins the current one. A pinned tab remembers its home page: right-click it and choose Go Home when you have wandered off.
  • Today: everything else. Tabs you have not touched for a while move to the archive, where History and the command bar can still find them. The delay is in Settings → General.
  • Spaces along the bottom edge, as coloured dots.

Windows: Cmd+N opens another window. Each window shows one space, and a space lives in one window at a time; right-click a space dot → Open in New Window. Windows and their positions come back at the next launch.

Subspaces group related tabs under a collapsible header inside a space: right-click a tab → Subspace → New Subspace from This Tab…, then drop other tabs onto the header. Some sites manage a subspace of their own, keeping one tab per task; they ask the first time.

Cmd+S collapses the sidebar; hover the left edge of the window to peek at it. Drag its right edge to resize it. Tabs that have not been used for a while go to sleep (greyed out) and wake on click.

The command bar

There is no address bar by default. Cmd+T opens the command bar for a new tab, Cmd+L opens it on the current tab's URL. Type any of:

  • a URL or a domain (bbc.co.uk),
  • words to search (the engine is in Settings → General),
  • the name of an open tab or a page from history to jump to it,
  • a command: Settings, Downloads, History, Import from another browser, Picture in Picture, Research or AI mode, and so on.

Press Enter to open in the current tab and Cmd+Enter for a new one. If you would rather see the URL all the time, Cmd+Shift+L shows a classic address bar above the page.

Spaces

A space is a sidebar of its own, with its own colour and icon. Cmd+Shift+N creates one. Switch with Ctrl+1…Ctrl+9 or Cmd+Alt+Left / Cmd+Alt+Right, or click the dots. Right-click a dot for everything else:

  • Rename, colour, icon: the colour tints the whole window, so you always know where you are.
  • Profile: give a space its own cookies and logins, for a second account or a client's systems. Spaces with the same profile name share them.
  • Private: lock the space with a password. It locks on launch, when the window is hidden and after fifteen minutes away. Its tabs never appear in history or exports.
  • Modes: Research mode adds a side panel for notes, clips and Zotero; AI mode adds a model that can read and act on the space's tabs; Sources mode checks who is behind every link of an article and which claims rest on nothing (with the Sourceror server running on your computer).
  • Routes (Air Traffic Control): in Settings → Routes, send links from chosen sites into this space, wherever they come from. Right-click a page → “Route this site to a Space…”.
  • Notes and easels: every space has a Markdown notes page (⌘⇧M) and freeform easels (⌘⇧E) for sticky notes, links, clipped text and page snapshots. Right-click a page for “Easel”.
  • Export the space as JSON to share it or move it to another machine.

Split view

Cmd+Shift+D adds the current tab to a split beside the one you are looking at; Cmd+Alt+W takes it out again. Drag the divider to resize. With three or more panes, Tabs → Split Layout offers grids such as two on top and one below, and Settings → Layouts lets you define your own with a short spec: 50/50 | 100 means a row of two equal panes above a full-width one.

Bring your tabs and bookmarks

File → Import from Another Browser… finds Arc, Chrome, Brave, Edge and Chromium on this machine and turns their bookmarks (and Arc's spaces, favorites and pinned tabs) into Chord spaces. Nothing is deleted from the other browser. Passwords are not imported: Chord offers to save them again as you sign in.

Settings → General can also export the whole configuration as one JSON file and import it on another computer.

Pages that talk back

Some pages get controls in the sidebar, visible from every space: a card with play, pause, next and volume for YouTube Music, Spotify and other players; a card with mute, camera and leave for Meet, Zoom and Jitsi calls; unread counts and timers read from page titles. With Settings → General → Keep running when the window is closed, those controls also live in the menu bartray.

When something is off

Help → Copy Diagnostics puts a report on the clipboard: versions, GPU state, page timings and the focused page's console errors. Paste it into an issue at github.com/sirmmo/chord/issues. Settings → General → Troubleshooting has switches for black videos and drawing glitches. Your profile folder is shown at the bottom of Settings → About.
